\section{Does the failure type decide the repair? (E4)}
\label{sec:e4}

We re-query all erroneous value questions in synthetic pilots 1 and 2 (631
for Qwen, 1,015 for LLaVA) to test whether the best repair depends on failure
type. Types come from the original answers, independently of probes: misreads
of shown bars ($S$), invented nonzero values for missing bars ($F$), and
missing-bar ``0'' answers ($Z$). We score $Z$ as wrong, separately from
fabrication.

We compare four repairs: a sampled re-ask of the original question
(temperature 0.7); whole-chart upsampling; a question-conditioned crop retaining
the target bar and y-axis strip; and a verification prompt requesting chart
evidence before the final answer. The latter three use greedy decoding.
Upsample and crop share a 1,000,000-pixel budget. Recovery requires a correct
read under the original 5\% numeric tolerance, or an explicit refusal for a
missing bar. The re-ask controls for improvement from another attempt.

% Sources: results/e4.md; results/e4-preregistration.md;
% src/intervene/interventions.py; src/intervene/matrix.py.
Before running the interventions, we pre-registered the difference in recovery
gains between $S$ and $F$ as the primary contrast on 2 September:
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\mathrm{crop}}=
 (r_{S,\mathrm{crop}}-r_{S,0})-(r_{F,\mathrm{crop}}-r_{F,0}),
\end{equation}
where $r$ denotes recovery rate. Baseline recovery is zero because we select
only errors; the contrast excludes $Z$. We use 2,000 figure-bootstrap
resamples for 95\% confidence intervals (CIs).

The crop DiDs are +0.261 for Qwen and +0.152 for LLaVA, with CIs excluding
zero as predicted (Table~\ref{tab:e4}). The pre-registered secondary verify
DiD is $-0.056$ [$-0.162$, +0.053] for Qwen, including zero, and +0.031
[+0.015, +0.050] for LLaVA.

Qwen's best repair is crop for misreads and verify for fabrications and $Z$
answers, recovering 14.3\% and 69.2\% respectively. Misreads and fabrications
select different best repairs in 99\% of bootstrap resamples. LLaVA's crop
helps misreads, but no repair recovers any of its 600 missing-bar errors.
All repairs tie on these errors, making an argmax change uninformative.
Re-asking turns 215 of its 232 $Z$ answers into invented numbers.

Crop falls back to upsampling when the bar is missing. The two repairs give
identical answers on all 1,185 missing-bar errors across models, so the primary
contrast cannot isolate localisation. Comparing crop with upsample isolates
this gain: +0.145 [0.111, 0.181] for LLaVA and +0.130 [$-0.023$, 0.289] for
Qwen. Qwen's interval includes zero, with only 46 misreads available.

\section{A type-aware controller (E5)}
\label{sec:e5}

% Sources: results/e5.md; src/controller/{e5,e5_scores}.py.
We compare a type-aware controller with binary selective prediction on all
1,200 value questions per model, including correct reads and refusals. Three
linear probes on the last prompt-token hidden state score wrong versus right,
missing versus shown bar, and misread versus correct read before generation.
Scores use five figure folds: each probe trains on the other four, selecting
layer and regularisation within its training folds.

The \emph{controller} re-looks above a misread-score threshold and withholds
questions with the highest missing-bar scores first. We choose the repair and
threshold to maximise accuracy on four folds, then apply them to the fifth.
Baselines include the plain model, binary abstention,
missing-bar abstention, and \emph{Re-look + binary}, which uses
the controller's re-look rule with binary ranking. The label-aware oracle
chooses the best action for each true class and withholds the least recoverable
classes first. Re-query outcomes on initially correct answers count harmful
repairs against each policy: crop breaks 133 of Qwen's 554 correct reads.

Coverage is the fraction retained; risk is their error rate. AURC averages
risk over coverage (lower is better), averaging tied confidence scores.
Correct refusals count as correct answers; policy abstention removes questions
from coverage. CIs use 1,000 figure-bootstrap resamples.

Adding a re-look lowers AURC under either abstention ranking in both models
(Table~\ref{tab:e5}, Figure~\ref{fig:e5}; all four gain CIs exclude zero).
The controller beats
binary abstention only for Qwen: AURC difference $-0.017$
[$-0.028$, $-0.004$], compared with +0.069 [0.045, 0.094] for LLaVA.
Without re-looking, binary ranking also outperforms missing-bar ranking
because it flags misreads as well as missing-bar errors. Re-look + binary
has the lowest AURC of the evaluated probe-based policies in both models
(0.114 and 0.638).

Qwen selects verify in every fold. Of 222 fixes, 212 are $Z$ answers
converted to refusals, seven are fabrications, and only three are misreads;
one correct refusal becomes wrong. Most gains are refusals on missing-bar
questions, although the re-look was intended to correct misreads.
The misread probe also responds to missing bars and therefore triggers this
repair. LLaVA selects crop in every fold. It fixes 12 misreads and re-looks
at 437 missing-bar questions without recovering any. With access to true
classes, the oracle achieves lower AURCs of 0.056 and 0.497, so better
policies are possible within this evaluation.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

% Sources: results/probes/binary_*_test.md;
% results/chartqa_type_probes.md; results/synth_type_probes.md;
% results/probes/{synth_grid_baseline,transfer_chartqa}_*.json.
E1 predicts wrong answers (AUROC 0.877 for Qwen, 0.798 for LLaVA; surface
baselines 0.715 and 0.608). Real-chart probes distinguish misreads from
computation errors (0.897 and 0.961), including after surface residualisation
(0.918 and 0.920). This partly supports type separability for a different
pair from the proposed misread/fabrication contrast. Question wording may
still explain part of the separation: the surface controls do not remove
question type.

The synthetic results depend on the model. Qwen's misread probe responds more
strongly to misreads (0.887) than fabrications (0.594); LLaVA's responds more
to fabrications (0.879) than misreads (0.776). Post-hoc gridline features with
chart metadata exceed both probes on misreads (0.920 and 0.860). Synthetic
misread probes also transfer weakly to ChartQA (0.597 and 0.557). We therefore
cannot infer a general mechanism shared by the models from separability alone.

% Source: results/pilot3_probes.md.
In pilot 3, the missing-versus-shown probes trained on absent category names
detect out-of-range years (AUROC 1.000 and 0.911). Qwen's probe barely separates
fabrication from correct refusal (0.521): its score tracks whether the chart supports answering, with
little separation between these responses. Qwen refuses 102 of 300
out-of-range questions, compared with 15 of 600 absent-name questions.
Its response differs between kinds of unsupported questions; chart design
also changes between these pilots.

Repair effects depend on failure type in E4, yet the type-aware controller
in E5 improves on binary abstention only for Qwen, mainly by prompting
refusals. For LLaVA's missing-bar errors, policy abstention is the only
effective option we tested. These results favour using answerability together
with a general error score, and assessing the repair and abstention ranking
as parts of the same policy.
