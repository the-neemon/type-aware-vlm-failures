\section*{Limitations, Challenges and Future Work}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{Labels.} The ChartQA labels come from one LLM annotator. Two human
annotators agreed with each other below our 0.6 gate, so the five-way taxonomy is
hard to apply consistently, and the adjudicated human labels match the LLM labels
only moderately. Type probes trained on these labels inherit their noise.

\paragraph{Scope of the type contrast.} Fabrication is nearly absent from
ChartQA, so the misread versus fabrication contrast rests on synthetic bar charts
with a single value-question template, where a fabrication always concerns
something missing. Synthetic misreads are partly determined by where a value sits
relative to the gridlines, the best gridline features were chosen after seeing
the data, and the synthetic probes do not carry over to real charts. Qwen makes
few misreads (46 synthetic, 71 real), so its intervals are wide. The question-type
rule is a keyword heuristic.

\paragraph{Repairs and the controller.} E4 and E5 use synthetic charts only,
because the crop needs bar geometry that ChartQA does not provide. Their balance
of shown and missing bars differs from how often unsupported questions occur in
real use, so the controller's risk-coverage numbers should not be extrapolated to
ChartQA. Cross-fitting by figure still reuses the same pilots, and we did not
optimise latency or inference cost. All probes are linear and diagnostic; we did
not test whether steering along the missing-bar direction changes the answer.

\paragraph{Future work.} Repair selection on naturalistic unanswerable
questions; a crop for real charts based on text detection; more models and chart
types, including node-link diagrams; and a causal test of the missing-bar
direction.
