\section{Labelling the ChartQA Errors}
\label{sec:labels}

On the 2,500 ChartQA test questions (1,250 human-written, 1,250 machine-augmented,
1,509 charts), Qwen is correct on 87.3\% and LLaVA on 52.9\%, leaving 317 and
1,178 wrong answers. Every wrong answer was labelled with five classes:
\cls{structural} (explained by misreading the figure), \cls{fabrication} (content
the figure does not support), \cls{computation} (inputs read correctly,
arithmetic or logic wrong), \cls{not\_an\_error} (actually right: an equivalent
format, a wrong gold answer, or a valid reading of the question) and
\cls{ambiguous}. An LLM annotator (Claude) labelled all errors from the chart,
question, gold and model answer, never seeing the model's identity, following a
written rubric (Appendix~\ref{app:examples} gives examples). Before labelling
LLaVA it re-labelled 50 random Qwen errors blind and agreed on 47
($\kappa=0.917$).

Table~\ref{tab:labels} gives three findings. \textbf{Fabrication is almost
absent from ChartQA} (1 of 317 and 17 of 1,178 errors): its questions are written
about content the chart contains, so a model rarely has occasion to invent, and
most apparent inventions turned out to be garbled printed values (486 for a
printed 4,863), which are misreads. \textbf{35\% of Qwen's scored errors are not
errors} (6\% of LLaVA's), mostly equivalent formats (0.95 for 95\%) and wrong gold
answers. \textbf{The models fail differently}: half of LLaVA's errors are
misreads, while Qwen's real errors are mostly arithmetic. The naturalistic arm
therefore supports a misread versus computation contrast, and fabrication needs
the synthetic arm (Section~\ref{sec:synth}).

\paragraph{Human agreement.} Two team members labelled the same 200 errors
blind (100 per model, stratified by the LLM label). They agreed with each other at
$\kappa=0.421$ (Qwen 0.452, LLaVA 0.345), below our 0.6 gate. After they
adjudicated the 79 disagreements together, the human labels agree with the LLM
labels at $\kappa=0.614$ overall (Qwen 0.589, LLaVA 0.613; 72\% raw agreement).
All experiments use the LLM labels.
