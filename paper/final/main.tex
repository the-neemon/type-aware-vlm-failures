\begin{abstract}
A vision-language model that answers a chart question wrongly may have misread
the figure or stated something the figure does not show. We ask whether these
failure types are distinguishable in the model's internal state before it
answers, and whether the type should decide the repair. On ChartQA, a linear
probe on the last prompt token of Qwen2.5-VL-7B and LLaVA-NeXT-7B predicts wrong
answers with AUROC 0.88 and 0.80, well above surface features of the question.
Fabrication is almost absent from real chart errors. The real errors are misreads
and arithmetic slips, which probes separate (0.90 and 0.96), also within a single
question type. On synthetic bar charts that ask about missing categories, neither
model refuses, yet both encode the absence (AUROC 0.96 to 1.00), and the same
signal flags years outside a chart's range. In a pre-registered repair
experiment, cropping to the asked bar rescues misreads more than fabrications in
both models. A type-aware controller beats binary selective prediction only for
Qwen, mainly by turning missing-bar answers into refusals. Whether a chart can
answer the question is the most useful internal signal for abstention, while
misread signals on synthetic charts largely reflect chart geometry.
\end{abstract}
