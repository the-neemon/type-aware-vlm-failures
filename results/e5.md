## Read these before writing them up

1. **Re-looking pays for Qwen, but not through misreads.** Of the controller's
   222 fixes, 212 are "0" answers to a missing bar that `I_verify` turns into an
   explicit refusal; only 3 are misreads. The cross-fitted rule picked
   `I_verify` in every fold, not `I_crop`: on Qwen a crop breaks one right
   answer in four (E5 re-ask), so a crop on a false alarm costs more than it
   saves. The gain is "ask Qwen to verify and it refuses", which is a repair of
   the missing-bar type, triggered here by the misread probe because that probe
   also fires on missing bars (E3: 0.673 on Z vs C).
2. **For LLaVA, re-looking adds almost nothing** (AURC -0.007 to -0.010). The
   rule picks `I_crop` and fixes 12 of 415 misreads, but also re-looks 437
   missing-bar questions and fixes none of them: no repair rescues a LLaVA
   missing-bar answer (E4).
3. **Abstention order matters more than the re-look.** Abstaining on the binary
   probe beats abstaining on the missing-bar probe for both models (Qwen 0.179
   vs 0.208, LLaVA 0.645 vs 0.725): the binary probe also catches misreads,
   which the missing-bar probe cannot. So the best non-oracle policy is
   `relook_binary` (Qwen 0.114, LLaVA 0.638), and the decision-7 controller,
   which abstains on the missing-bar probe, beats binary for Qwen (-0.017) but
   loses to it for LLaVA (+0.069).
4. **The oracle gap is large** (Qwen 0.056, LLaVA 0.497): knowing the true type
   would buy much more than the probes deliver, mostly because the oracle never
   re-looks a right answer and abstains first on the types no repair can fix.
5. The universe is the synthetic value questions, where half the questions ask
   about a missing bar; ChartQA has almost no missing-bar questions, so these
   AURCs do not transfer to real charts.
